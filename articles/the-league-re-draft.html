4 Weeks Later… Who Actually Won Draft Night?

SPECIAL EDITION | MATCHUP 5 | NOVEMBER 2026

14 managers. 56 picks under the microscope. Four completed matchups. And a whole lot of draft-night decisions that deserve a second look.

THE RECEIPTS ARE IN 📸

October 18, 2026.

Fourteen managers showed up to THE LEAGUE draft convinced they were building championship teams.

Some drafted superstars. Others trusted their research. A few went with their gut. And at least one manager probably picked someone because ESPN had a nice projection next to their name.

Well, four weeks later, we have some questions.

Because if we held that same draft today, knowing what we know now, things would look VERY different.

Who found the biggest steal? Who wasted a premium pick? And which manager actually walked away with the best draft class?

Let's look at the evidence.

👑 THE KING OF THE DRAFT: WEMBY TAKES THE THRONE?

Nikola Jokić went first overall.

Giannis went second. Luka third. And Victor Wembanyama landed with Minneapolis Lakers at No. 4.

Fast-forward to our current scoring snapshot:

PlayerDraft pickFantasy points
Victor Wembanyama4570
Giannis Antetokounmpo2520
Luka Dončić3510
Nikola Jokić1476

Wemby leads this group in both total fantasy points and points per game.

Does that mean Jokić was a bad first pick? Absolutely not.

But if we were drafting again based on what we've seen so far, Wemby would have a very strong argument for No. 1.

Minneapolis Lakers might have landed the best player in the draft without owning the first pick.

💎 THE STEAL OF THE DRAFT: FRANZ WAGNER AT 55?!

Let's talk about the pick that might deserve a police investigation.

Franz Wagner.

Drafted 55th overall by DX ✌️ two words SUC...

Current cumulative scoring position: 9th.

That's 46 spots above his original draft position.

We're talking about a player selected near the end of the fourth round producing more total fantasy points than most of the players taken before him.

And Wagner isn't the only bargain.

Anthony Davis was drafted 32nd by Minneapolis Lakers and has produced 416 fantasy points.

Karl-Anthony Towns went 16th to Ringo's Cool Team and has delivered 444.

Alperen Şengün went 18th to El TumbaCuello and has delivered 436.

Some managers paid first-round prices for first-round talent.

Others found it at a discount.

That's how championships are built.

🚨 THE BUST WATCH: SOME PICKS ARE AGING LIKE MILK

Now for the uncomfortable part.

Not every early pick has lived up to expectations.

Jalen Johnson was selected seventh overall and sits at 317 cumulative fantasy points in our snapshot.

Trae Young, drafted 34th, has produced 308.

And several other premium picks are missing from the top of the scoring leaderboard.

But here's where things get complicated.

Injuries matter.

A player missing games isn't necessarily playing badly. However, from a fantasy perspective, a first-round pick sitting on the bench can be just as painful as one putting up disappointing numbers.

That's why we're separating our draft disappointments into two categories:

The Underperformers: Players whose production hasn't justified their draft position.

The Injury Casualties: Players whose fantasy value has been limited by availability.

Different circumstances. Same frustrated fantasy managers.

And yes, we'll be keeping the receipts.

🏆 WHO ACTUALLY HAD THE BEST DRAFT?

This is the question that will probably start the biggest argument in the group chat.

A few teams have already made strong cases.

Minneapolis Lakers 🏆 landed Wembanyama at No. 4 and Anthony Davis at No. 32. That's an impressive combination of elite production and draft value.

El TumbaCuello 🏆🏆 grabbed Şengün at No. 18, getting top-six cumulative production from a second-round pick.

Ringo's Cool Team found Karl-Anthony Towns at No. 16, another player producing well above his original draft slot.

And DX ✌️ two words SUC... deserves serious consideration for finding Franz Wagner at No. 55.

But one great pick doesn't automatically mean one great draft.

This isn’t a final verdict—four weeks don’t decide a season. But the early receipts are in, and some picks are already looking very different.

Who won draft night? Who found the biggest steal? And who deserves a little more time before we judge?

THE LEAGUE, let the debate begin. 🏀🔥

🔄 THE RE-DRAFT: WOULD YOU MAKE THE SAME PICK?

Imagine we could rewind to October 18.

Same 14 managers. Same draft order.

But everyone knows what happened during the first four matchups.

Does Wemby go first?

Does Franz Wagner suddenly become a first-round pick?

How far does Anthony Davis climb?

And which first-round selections would managers think twice about?

That's the real question behind THE LEAGUE Re-Draft.

We're not trying to predict the entire season.

We're asking something much more entertaining:

Knowing what you know today, would you draft the same team again?
